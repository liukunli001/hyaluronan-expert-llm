## Abstract

Specialized scientific fields accumulate knowledge in large, dispersed
literatures that general-purpose language models handle unreliably: answers
are fluent but often wrong on quantitative details, and confident even when
the premise of a question is false. We study one such field — hyaluronan
(hyaluronic acid, HA), a glycosaminoglycan whose research spans glycochemistry,
biomaterials, drug delivery, and clinical medicine — and ask how far a single
researcher with a modest compute budget can go in building a *trustworthy*
domain model. We release (i) a cleaned corpus of 12,306 full-text HA papers
(~126M tokens); (ii) HA-Expert, a Qwen3-8B-Base model adapted in two stages —
domain-adaptive continued pretraining (DAPT) on 60M tokens of the corpus,
followed by full-parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on 12,003
question–answer pairs generated by the DAPT model itself, at zero API cost —
and optionally grounded at inference time by retrieval over a 636,959-passage
FAISS index of the same corpus; and (iii) HA-Expert-Bench, 240 questions whose
reference answers were verified against retrieved source passages by
evidence-restricted LLM verifiers (212 of 240 keys confirmed or corrected
through this process), including 15 adversarial "trap" questions with false
premises. In a four-cell ablation, SFT doubles the base model (0.300 → 0.601);
retrieval is the largest single lever (base+RAG 0.710; traps 0.267 → 0.867);
and, notably, SFT and RAG do **not** compose additively (0.662): a model that
never saw retrieved context in training collapses into citation-marker loops
on 5 of 240 retrieval-augmented responses, and inherits its teacher's
repetition habit through self-distillation. Total cloud compute for the full
pipeline was under USD 50. All code, data, weights, and the benchmark are
publicly released.

## 1. Introduction

Hyaluronan is a linear glycosaminoglycan of repeating
β-1,4-glucuronic-acid / β-1,3-N-acetylglucosamine disaccharides, central to
extracellular-matrix biology, joint lubrication, wound healing, ophthalmic
and dermal formulations, and an expanding drug-delivery literature. The
knowledge a practitioner needs — molecular-weight-dependent biology, receptor
interactions (CD44, RHAMM, LYVE-1), chemical modification routes, production
methods, clinical dosing — is spread across thousands of papers in chemistry,
materials, and medical journals. No single textbook tracks it.

General-purpose LLMs are an attractive interface to such a literature, but in
our initial probes the failure modes were systematic rather than occasional:
the base model we started from frequently *continued the prompt with invented
follow-up questions instead of answering*, assented to false premises
(e.g., that HA is a protein polymer, or that HA solutions are Newtonian
fluids), and produced quantitative claims without any checkable source.

This paper is a case study in fixing those failure modes with the standard
toolkit — continued pretraining, instruction tuning, retrieval — under three
constraints that we believe are typical for individual researchers and small
labs: (1) a single rented GPU and a total budget under USD 50; (2) no paid
teacher model — all supervision is self-generated and then filtered; and
(3) evaluation that a domain expert can trust, which for us meant reference
answers that are *checked against source text*, not merely model-written.

Contributions:

- **Corpus.** A documented, decontaminated corpus of 12,306 open-access HA
  papers (~126M tokens), with 30 review papers held out from every stage.
- **Model.** HA-Expert (Qwen3-8B-Base + DAPT + SFT), plus a retrieval mode
  with inline citations; weights, a DAPT checkpoint, and a public demo are
  released.
- **Benchmark.** HA-Expert-Bench: 240 questions with an evidence-verification
  pipeline for the answer keys (two rounds: keyword-evidence, then
  vector-evidence re-verification), and an explicit accounting of how many
  keys are confirmed, corrected, or still uncertain.
- **Ablations and failure analysis.** A four-cell study (base / +SFT / +RAG /
  +both) with per-type scores, a trap-by-trap table, and a documented
  interference effect between SFT and RAG with a concrete remediation path.

## 2. Corpus

We harvested records from PubMed Central's open-access subset using
hyaluronan-related queries, obtaining 16,642 records, of which 12,945 had
retrievable full text (JATS XML). Cleaning removed navigation boilerplate,
reference lists were retained as text, exact- and near-duplicate documents
were dropped, and documents were language- and length-filtered, leaving
**12,306 papers, ≈126M tokens**.

**Holdout.** Thirty review papers were set aside before any processing:
they are excluded from the DAPT sets, from the passages used to generate SFT
data, and from the retrieval index, so that benchmark questions derived from
them measure generalization rather than recall.

**DAPT sets.** The continued-pretraining data comprises a *core set* of
4,952 papers (60.0M tokens) and a 498-paper *anneal set* (5.7M tokens) of
high-quality reviews and methods papers placed at the end of training, mixed
with 12% general-domain replay text (FineWeb-Edu) against catastrophic
forgetting. The corpus and manifests are released (GitHub Release v0.1-data).

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT)

Starting from Qwen3-8B-Base, we ran full-parameter continued pretraining
with documents chunked at document boundaries to a sequence length of 4,096
(legacy block packing, SDPA attention), batch 8 × gradient accumulation 16
(524,288 tokens/step), learning rate 1e-5 with cosine decay. Training ran
212 steps (≈111M tokens processed) in 5 h 34 min on a single RTX PRO 6000
(96 GB), reaching a final training loss of ≈1.55. DAPT alone did not teach
answering behavior — on a 63-question development split it tied the base
model on auto-graded items (5/6) while repairing knowledge traps — which
motivated the SFT stage.

### 3.2 Self-generated SFT data

Paying for a frontier teacher was out of budget, so the DAPT model served
as its own teacher. We served it locally with vLLM and, for passages sampled
from the corpus, generated question–answer pairs conditioned on the passage.
Pairs were kept only if the answer was *grounded* in its source passage
(content-word overlap ≥ 0.55) and deduplicated; two generation rounds
(5,403 + 6,600 pairs) yielded **12,003 examples** at zero API cost.

SFT was full-parameter (lr 1e-5, 2 epochs, 752 steps, prompt/completion
format with completion-only loss, NEFTune noise 5.0, 8-bit Adam), finishing
at train loss 1.214 in ≈19 minutes. Qualitatively, the base model's
signature pathology — inventing follow-up questions instead of answering —
disappeared from SFT outputs.

### 3.3 Retrieval-augmented inference (RAG)

All 12,306 papers were chunked (paragraphs merged to ≈900 characters,
150-character overlap on long paragraphs) and embedded with
Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (1,024-d), giving a FAISS IndexFlatIP index of
**636,959 passages**. At inference the top-4 passages are prepended to the
question with citation markers [1]–[4] and the model is instructed to cite
them. Holdout papers are absent from the index.

## 4. HA-Expert-Bench

**Composition.** 240 questions in seven content categories (basics,
history/industry, synthesis & chemistry, modification/derivatives,
molecular-weight biology & receptors, nano-delivery, experimental/clinical),
in four formats: open (195), quantitative (20), multiple-choice (10), and
*trap* (15). Trap questions embed a false premise a knowledgeable expert
should reject (e.g., "HA solutions are simple Newtonian fluids, correct?").

**Evidence-checked keys.** Draft keys were not trusted as written. In round
one, LLM verifiers received evidence packs assembled by tracing each
question to its source paper (38) or by keyword retrieval over the corpus
(202) and could only confirm, correct (citing the decisive passage), or mark
the key *uncertain*: 118 verified, 19 corrected, 103 uncertain. In round
two, the 103 uncertain keys were re-verified against top-6 passages from
the vector index (Section 3.3): 72 verified, 3 corrected, 28 still
uncertain. The released key set (v3) thus has **212/240 keys
evidence-settled**, and every per-question status is published.

**Grading.** MCQ items are graded by exact option letter and quantitative
items by numeric tolerance (auto-graded). Open and trap items are graded by
an LLM judge shown the question, the verified reference answer, and the
response, returning correct / partial / wrong (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.0). The judge
was the SFT model itself, served locally — a deliberate $0 choice and a
known limitation (Section 6); anchoring to a fixed reference key rather
than free pairwise preference mitigates, but does not eliminate,
self-judging bias. All decoding was greedy, max 512 tokens.

## 5. Results

| Cell | Overall | MCQ (10) | Quant. (20) | Open (195) | Trap (15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B-Base | 0.300 | 0.800 | 0.250 | 0.282 | 0.267 |
| + DAPT & SFT | 0.601 | 0.900 | 0.300 | 0.631 | 0.500 |
| + RAG | **0.710** | 0.900 | 0.450 | 0.719 | **0.867** |
| + DAPT & SFT & RAG | 0.662 | 0.700 | **0.550** | 0.676 | 0.633 |

*Table 1. HA-Expert-Bench (240 questions). Residual ungraded items (judge
output unparseable even after a retry pass): base 13, SFT 23, base+RAG 2,
SFT+RAG 21; overall scores are means over graded items. On the 30
auto-graded items alone: 0.433 / 0.500 / 0.600 / 0.600.*

**SFT teaches behavior; DAPT supplies knowledge.** Fine-tuning doubled the
base model overall and more than doubled open-question accuracy
(0.282 → 0.631), by converting a text completer into a system that answers
the question asked. DAPT's contribution is visible in knowledge traps it
repairs (e.g., rejecting "HA is a protein") already before SFT.

**Retrieval is the largest single lever.** Adding RAG to the *base* model
beats the full trained model without it (0.710 vs 0.601), and is dramatic
exactly where parametric knowledge is least reliable: trap accuracy rises
0.267 → 0.867. Both long-standing trap failures of the parametric models
(G01, G13) are fixed by both RAG cells.

**SFT and RAG interfere.** The combination underperforms base+RAG on open
(0.676 vs 0.719) and trap (0.633 vs 0.867) questions, while achieving the
best quantitative score (0.550). Output inspection gives a mechanism, not
just a number: the SFT model was trained only on bare question→answer
pairs and never saw retrieved-context prompts; on 5 of 240 RAG inputs it
collapses into loops of citation markers ("[1] [4] [2] [3] …") on questions
its SFT-only counterpart answers correctly. Separately, self-distillation
inherited the teacher's habits: degenerate repetition (a 30-character
block repeated ≥5×) appears in 35/240 SFT responses vs 10/240 for the
base model. Both failure modes have concrete fixes — RAG-format SFT
examples, repetition filtering of teacher outputs — which we leave as
documented future work rather than silently re-running until the table
looks nicer.

## 6. Case studies

**G13 — "HA solutions are simple Newtonian fluids, so their viscosity is a
single constant, correct?"**
The SFT model assents and then repeats itself: *"Yes, HA solutions are
simple Newtonian fluids, so their viscosity is a single constant."* The
same model with retrieval corrects the premise: *"No, HA solutions are not
simple Newtonian fluids. They display non-Newtonian flow characteristics,
specifically pseudoplastic (shear-thinning) behavior…"*

**G09 — "For joint injections, the highest-MW HA is always the best
choice, correct?"**
The SFT model correctly rejects the absolute ("…depends on the specific
condition being treated…"), while the SFT+RAG response degenerates
entirely into a citation-marker loop — the interference failure of
Section 5 in its purest form, on a question the parametric model gets
right.

## 7. Discussion and limitations

**On the benchmark.** Evidence-checking the keys changed 22 of 240 of
them across two rounds; benchmarks built from unchecked model-written
keys would silently carry that error rate into every score. 28 keys
remain uncertain and are flagged as such in the release. We re-scored
the three v3-corrected items explicitly: no verdict changed in any cell,
so Table 1 is unaffected.

**Limitations.** (i) The judge is one of the contestants; gaps below
≈0.02 should be read as noise, and an external judge is the obvious
upgrade. (ii) MCQ n=10 — the SFT+RAG dip there is two questions.
(iii) Self-generated SFT data can encode teacher errors despite grounding
filters; the 0.55 overlap threshold checks topical grounding, not truth.
(iv) Single seed, greedy decoding; repetition behavior is
decode-sensitive. (v) 8B scale and one narrow domain: the pipeline, not
any absolute score, is the transferable claim.

**Replicability.** Nothing in the pipeline is HA-specific beyond the
corpus query: harvest → clean → DAPT → self-generated grounded SFT →
evidence-checked bench → four-cell ablation, at a total measured cloud
cost of roughly USD 35 (single-GPU rental; all other services free
tiers). For small fields whose literature is open-access, we believe
this is a realistic template for a first trustworthy domain model — and
the interference result is a warning worth having before, not after,
combining SFT with retrieval.
