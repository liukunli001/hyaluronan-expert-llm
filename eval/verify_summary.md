## Corrections (19)

1. **A09** (basics/quantitative)
   - Old: Up to about 1,000 times its weight in water (commonly cited figure).
   - New: HA can bind 10 to 10,000 times more water than its own weight.
   - Why: Evidence PMC12114106 states HA is 'able to bind 10 to 10,000 times more water than its weight', so the draft's 'up to about 1,000 times' understates the evidenced range.
2. **A15** (basics/open)
   - Old: HAS2 produces the longest chains (typically >2 MDa); HAS1 and HAS3 produce shorter chains (roughly 0.2-2 MDa), with HAS3 generally the most active enzyme per molecule.
   - New: HAS1 and HAS2 produce longer HA chains than HAS3 (Has1 = Has2 > Has3); HAS3 produces lower-mass HA (e.g., <200,000 Da), and HAS2 is the most active and highly expressed of the three isoforms.
   - Why: Evidence PMC3360468 states 'the average polymer length of HA synthesized by each type of Has enzyme is different (Has1 = Has2 > Has3)' with lower-mass HA '(<200,000 Da), such as that produced by Has3', and PMC4758074 states 'HAS2 is the most active and highly expressed' - contradicting the draft's HAS2-only longest chains, its grouped 0.2-2 MDa range, and HAS3 being most active.
3. **B12** (receptors_signaling/quantitative)
   - Old: About 80-95 kDa as a mature glycoprotein (core protein ~37 kDa, heavily glycosylated).
   - New: About 85-90 kDa for the standard CD44 (sCD44).
   - Why: Draft's 80-95 kDa range and ~37 kDa core figure are not in the evidence; PMC5339222 states: "The standard CD44 (sCD44) is the conserved form with a molecular weight of about 85–90 kDa".
4. **B18** (receptors_signaling/open)
   - Old: HAS2-driven HA production and CD44 signaling upregulate EMT programs (e.g., Twist/Snail activity and loss of epithelial markers); HAS2 knockdown reverses the mesenchymal, invasive phenotype.
   - New: HAS2 acts in a ZEB1/ESRP1/HAS2/CD44 feedback circuit that drives epithelial-hybrid-mesenchymal transition: depleting HAS2 prevented the TGFbeta-induced epithelial-to-mesenchymal phenotype transition by about 50% and HAS2 knockdown inhibited cell migration - and in the TGFbeta model this role of HAS2 was independent of its hyaluronan-synthesizing activity.
   - Why: Draft attributes the effect to HA production and Twist/Snail, which the evidence does not support; PMC3778714 states: "the role of HAS2 in TGFβ-mediated EMT is independent of its hyaluronan synthesizing activity" and that "depletion of HAS2 prevented the transition of the epithelial cobble-stone phenotype to spindle-like mesenchymal phenotype by about 50%".
5. **C02** (mw_biology/open)
   - Old: Short HA oligosaccharides (roughly 4-25 disaccharides) are pro-angiogenic, opposite to native HMW-HA, which is anti-angiogenic.
   - New: Short HA oligosaccharides (roughly 3–10 disaccharides) are pro-angiogenic, opposite to native HMW-HA, which is anti-angiogenic.
   - Why: Evidence PMC5823590 states: "native high molecular-weight HA (HMW-HA) is anti-angiogenic" whereas "degradation products that are of specific size (e.g., 3–10 disaccharide units; o-HA) exhibit pro-angiogenic activity" — the evidenced oligosaccharide range is 3–10, not the draft's 4-25.
6. **C21** (mw_biology/open)
   - Old: Serum HA also rises with joint inflammation, renal impairment, and other states of increased production or reduced clearance - it is sensitive but not liver-specific.
   - New: Serum HA is misleading because, as one of the four liver-fibrosis markers, it is sensitive but poorly specific: those markers also increase in renal fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis and other organ fibrosis, and are also affected by bone growth, drugs, etc.
   - Why: Draft's joint-inflammation and renal-impairment examples are not in the evidence; the decisive fragments are: "The four items of liver fiber not only increase in liver fibrosis, but also in renal fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis and other organ fibrosis" and they are "affected by bone growth, drugs, etc.", with "the disadvantages of high sensitivity and poor specificity" (PMC9843355).
7. **C22** (mw_biology/open)
   - Old: Fragment-rich tumor HA promotes angiogenesis (oligosaccharides stimulate endothelial proliferation/migration), feeding tumor growth, while intact HMW-HA is anti-angiogenic - tumors benefit from partial degradation.
   - New: The evidence does not support a fragment-pro-angiogenic versus HMW-HA-anti-angiogenic dichotomy: HMW-HA itself was reported to increase angiogenesis by promoting endothelial cell proliferation, migration and tube formation, and HA-CD44 signaling is linked to tumor progression and angiogenesis, while the exact mechanisms by which HA contributes to tumor angiogenesis are not yet well elucidated.
   - Why: The decisive fragments are: "HMW-HA increases endothelial cell proliferation, migration and tube formation" (PMC5823590) and "The exact mechanisms by which HA can contribute with tumor angiogenesis has not yet been well elucidated" (PMC4685440); no window supports oligosaccharide-driven angiogenesis or an anti-angiogenic HMW-HA.
8. **F06** (clinical_quant/quantitative)
   - Old: Roughly 0.5 to 6 MDa across products (standard preparations ~0.5-3 MDa; crosslinked hylan G-F 20 averages ~6 MDa).
   - New: Roughly 0.5 to >6 MDa across preparations (MW 500 kDa to >6,000 kDa).
   - Why: Draft's standard-preparation 0.5-3 MDa subrange and hylan G-F 20 average of ~6 MDa are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "MW (500 kDa to >6,000 kDa)" (PMC8808930).
9. **F08** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: About a day (skin half-life under 24 h; synovial residence under a day). Chemical crosslinking (e.g., BDDE) extends filler persistence to roughly 6-24 months.
   - New: Native, uncrosslinked HA has a half-life of a few days in human tissues, and chemical crosslinking (most commonly with BDDE) is used to increase filler lifetime in the body.
   - Why: Draft's under-24-hour skin half-life, sub-day synovial residence and 6-24 month filler duration are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "As it has a few days of half-life in human tissues, HA-based dermal filler is chemically modified to increase its lifetime in the body" (PMC6197218).
10. **F11** (clinical_quant/quantitative)
   - Old: About 10 mg/mL (1%) sodium hyaluronate of high MW (~4-5 MDa).
   - New: About 10 mg/mL (1%) sodium hyaluronate of high MW (~4 MDa).
   - Why: Draft's ~4-5 MDa upper figure is not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "Healon (sodium hyaluronate 1%, 4 x 10^6 daltons)" (PMC2698691).
11. **F12** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: Severe acute inflammatory joint reactions (pain, effusion) without infection, reported mainly with crosslinked avian-derived hylan G-F 20; they are immune/inflammatory reactions to the product, managed conservatively and by avoiding re-injection.
   - New: Severe acute inflammatory joint reactions (pseudosepsis): severe inflammation of the joint, often with significant effusion and significant pain, occurring after more than one injection and with infection ruled out; such reactions have been reported after intra-articular HA injections, including avian-derived hylan G-F 20.
   - Why: Draft's 'mainly with' hylan claim and conservative/re-injection management are not settled by the evidence (reports are conflicting and the avian allergic-type role was not supported); the decisive fragment is: "they defined pseudosepsis as having certain characteristics, which include severe inflammation of the joint, often with significant polymorphonuclear cellular effusion and significant pain" (PMC8808807).
12. **F13** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: A membrane of HA with carboxymethylcellulose (chemically modified), placed at surgery to reduce postoperative adhesions; it gels on contact with tissue and resorbs within about a week.
   - New: A bioresorbable membrane composed of chemically modified sodium hyaluronate (HA) and carboxymethylcellulose (CMC), placed at surgery as an adhesion barrier to reduce the incidence, extent, and severity of postoperative adhesions.
   - Why: Draft's gelling-on-contact and resorption-within-about-a-week details are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "Seprafilm adhesion barrier is a bioresorbable membrane composed of chemically modified sodium hyaluronate (HA) and carboxymethylcellulose (CMC)" (PMC3401301).
13. **F14** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: Cohesive OVDs are high-MW, high-viscosity HA preparations that hold together and are removed as a mass; dispersive OVDs are lower-MW and coat/protect tissues but are harder to remove completely.
   - New: Cohesive OVDs contain high-molecular-weight HA and protect intraocular tissues by maintaining a deep anterior chamber due to their high viscoelasticity; dispersive OVDs tend to adhere to tissue, resulting in greater corneal endothelial protection during phacoemulsification.
   - Why: Draft's hold-together/removed-as-a-mass and lower-MW/harder-to-remove claims are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "Cohesive OVDs containing high molecular weight HA protect intraocular tissues ... by maintaining a deep anterior chamber due to their high viscoelasticity" and dispersive OVDs "tend to adhere to tissue, resulting in greater corneal endothelial protection" (PMC12048296).
14. **F15** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: To replenish the bladder's protective glycosaminoglycan layer, whose loss exposes urothelium to irritants; HA (often with chondroitin sulfate) reduces pain, urgency, and frequency and can raise bladder capacity.
   - New: To replenish the bladder's protective glycosaminoglycan (GAG) layer, whose depletion makes the epithelium leaky and exposes the bladder wall to urine solutes and irritants; HA (often instilled with chondroitin sulfate) reduces bladder pain, urgency, and frequency.
   - Why: Draft's claim that HA can raise bladder capacity is not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "the GAG deficiency results in a 'leaky' epithelium, allowing the passage and influx of potassium ions, bacteria, and urine solutes towards the bladder tissue" (PMC11013000), and instillations of chondroitin and hyaluronic acid improved bladder pain, urgency, and frequency.
15. **P02** (basics/open)
   - Old: Fermentation avoids animal-source risks (viral/prion contamination, batch variability, ethical and supply limits) and gives controllable, scalable production of defined HA.
   - New: Microbial production was developed because animal-derived HA is difficult to control, high in cost, and raises ethical concerns associated with extraction from animal tissues such as rooster combs.
   - Why: Draft's viral/prion, batch-variability and supply-limit reasons are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "the difficulty in controlling animal tissue, high costs, and ethical concerns associated with animal-derived HA have led to the development of microbial production methods" (PMC10004376).
16. **P06** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: Absorbed HA fragments are proposed to act via receptor binding in the intestinal epithelia (e.g., TLR4-related signaling) and distribution to joints, modulating synovial inflammation - supported by RCT evidence for pain and effusion relief.
   - New: Oral HA is proposed to act via receptor binding in the intestinal epithelia, modulating inflammation via Toll-like receptor-4 signaling in intestinal epithelial cells (upregulating suppressor of cytokine signaling-3 and down-regulating pleiotrophin); randomized trials support relief of knee pain and synovial effusion/inflammation.
   - Why: Draft's 'absorbed fragments' and 'distribution to joints' are not in the evidence; the decisive fragment is: "The mechanism by which HA exerts its effects in the living body, specifically receptor binding in the intestinal epithelia, has gradually been clarified" (PMC4729158).
17. **P17** (chemistry_modification/open)
   - Old: Chitosan is the cationic thiomer, HA the anionic one; thiolated-HA nanoparticles are used notably for taxanes and siRNA delivery.
   - New: Chitosan is the cationic thiomer and HA is the anionic one; in the evidence, thiolated HA is used as a cross-linked anionic outer shell to protect, stabilize, and optimize an siRNA-loaded core (siRNA delivery).
   - Why: Draft's taxane payload is not in the evidence; the decisive fragments are: "cationic thiolated polymers are used, such as CTS-based thiomers" and "cross-linked thiolated HA was used to form an anionic outer shell to protect, stabilize, and optimize the siRNA-loaded core" (PMC8227107).
18. **P24** (hydrogel_scaffold/open)
   - Old: HA-enhanced grafts significantly reduced post-extraction bone resorption versus controls, with better radiographic bone density and less graft shrinkage (4 human studies in the review).
   - New: HA-enhanced grafts significantly reduced post-extraction bone resorption versus controls, with better radiographic bone density and less graft shrinkage.
   - Why: The '(4 human studies in the review)' count appears nowhere in the evidence; the supported findings are quoted: 'significantly reduced post-extraction bone resorption compared to controls' and 'increased bone density and reduced graft shrinkage'.
19. **P27** (clinical_quant/open)
   - Old: Mesenchymal stem cells combined with platelet-rich plasma and varying doses of HA (9 studies met inclusion), assessed on scores including Lysholm, VAS, KSS, and WOMAC.
   - New: Mesenchymal stem cells combined with platelet-rich plasma and varying doses of HA, assessed on scores including Lysholm, VAS, KSS, and WOMAC.
   - Why: The '(9 studies met inclusion)' count appears nowhere in the evidence; the supported combination is quoted: 'combining them with platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and hyaluronic acid (HA)' to assess 'synergy of MSCs, PRP, and varying HA doses' on Lysholm, VAS, KSS, and WOMAC scores.
